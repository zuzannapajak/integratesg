## Company overview

**Name:** Harmonica (brand of Bio Bulgaria AD)

**Sector:** Organic and natural food production (dairy, spreads, wafers, snacks, tahini and nut butters, jams, cordials, vinegar, honey)

**Location:** Sofia, Bulgaria, sourcing from farms across the country

**Size:** Small-to-medium enterprise (SME); founded 2006

**Reporting Year:** 2022–2024 (Certified B Corporation since September 2022)

Harmonica was founded in 2006 by Magdalena Maleeva (a former top-4 world tennis player turned climate activist), Lubomir Nokov (the company's current CEO) and Metodi Metodiev, beginning with organic yoghurt production on Bulgaria's first two organic cow farms. The company has since grown to a portfolio of more than 100 organic products, exported to over 20 countries, and in September 2022 became one of the first Bulgarian companies to achieve Certified B Corporation status.

## ESG integration

ESG integration at Harmonica is not treated as an external requirement but as the founding philosophy of the business, shaped through organic and regenerative farming practices, transparent supplier relationships, and a formal B Corp governance commitment adopted in 2022.

## Environmental

### Organic and regenerative sourcing

- Products are made from certified organic ingredients using regenerative farming methods, with minimal processing and clean labels free of artificial additives or preservatives

### Local supply chains

- Sourcing prioritises local Bulgarian farmers, reducing transport-related emissions and supporting regional agricultural ecosystems

### Packaging and biodiversity

- Ongoing work to reduce plastic use and improve recyclability, identified explicitly as a priority area following B Corp certification
- Participation in the "Clean Struma" (Чиста Струма) initiative, supporting water conservation and biodiversity restoration in the Struma river basin

## Social

### Farmer partnerships

- Long-term relationships with small Bulgarian farmers contribute to stable incomes and rural development

### Consumer transparency

- Clean labelling and open communication about ingredients and production processes reinforce consumer trust

### Workplace culture and advocacy

- Internally, the company promotes a collaborative work environment and shared responsibility for quality and sustainability outcomes
- Leadership actively promotes the B Corp movement in Bulgaria, including co-hosting the country's first "B Corp Café" event with fellow certified company Devin

## Governance

### Formal stakeholder governance

- Certified B Corporation since September 2022, one of the first in Bulgaria, requiring the formal integration of stakeholder considerations (workers, community, environment, customers) into governing documents
- Achieved a B Impact Assessment score of 117.1 — well above both the 80-point certification threshold and the 50.9 median score of ordinary businesses that complete the assessment

### Recertification and continuous improvement

- Certification must be renewed every three years against evolving B Lab standards, with the company having named packaging, emissions and farming-method improvements as explicit next goals

### Day-to-day governance

- Despite a lean SME structure, responsibility for sourcing, quality and sustainability standards is clearly assigned within management, ensuring consistency between stated values and daily operations

## Ratings and evidence

As a privately owned Bulgarian SME, Harmonica (via Bio Bulgaria AD) does not receive ratings from international ESG agencies such as MSCI or Sustainalytics. It does, however, hold a formal, publicly disclosed ESG-relevant score through its B Corp certification.

- B Impact Assessment overall score: 117.1 (B Lab's minimum qualifying score for certification is 80; the median for ordinary assessed businesses is 50.9)
- Recognised as a Great Taste Producer, with products having won British Great Taste Awards — an informal, quality-linked reputational indicator
- Organic certification and supplier audits function as ongoing operational proxies for ESG performance monitoring

## Regulatory compliance

Harmonica demonstrates consistent compliance with the regulatory frameworks governing organic food production, labelling and consumer protection, alongside voluntary compliance obligations tied to its B Corp status.

- EU Organic Production Regulation (EU) 2018/848 and corresponding Bulgarian organic certification requirements
- Bulgarian Food Safety Agency requirements and EU food safety and traceability law
- Labelling and consumer protection rules governing organic and "clean label" product claims
- B Lab's disclosure and risk review process, an additional voluntary layer of accountability beyond statutory requirements, verified on a three-year recertification cycle

## Recommendations

Structured annual impact reporting: Harmonica should publish a concise, structured annual sustainability or B Impact update — building on the packaging, emissions and farming-method goals it set at certification in 2022 — to track progress transparently for consumers, partners and financiers.

Formalised packaging circularity roadmap: The company should convert its stated intention to "do much more in packaging choices" into a documented roadmap with measurable milestones for plastic reduction and recyclability improvements.

Quantified farmer-partnership impact: Harmonica could formalise metrics on its farmer network — such as number of partner farms, hectares under organic or regenerative management, and income stability outcomes — to evidence its rural-development contribution more concretely.

Sector-level advocacy: Building on its co-hosted B Corp Café with Devin, Harmonica is well placed to help scale ESG adoption among Bulgarian SMEs by documenting and sharing its certification journey as a replicable model.

### Learning value

This case demonstrates an alternative route to ESG credibility for SMEs: rather than relying on investor-oriented ratings such as MSCI or Sustainalytics, Harmonica anchors its accountability in a voluntary, third-party-verified certification (B Corp) embedded directly into its governing structure. It offers a useful contrast to the regulated-utility model of Case Study 1, showing learners how founder-driven values, transparent supply relationships, and formal stakeholder-governance commitments can substitute for complex corporate ESG reporting systems in smaller, values-led businesses.
