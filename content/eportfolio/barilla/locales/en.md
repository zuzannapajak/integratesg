## Company overview

Barilla G. e R. Fratelli S.p.A. is one of the world’s leading food companies and one of the best-known Italian companies internationally. Founded in Parma in 1877, the Group specialises in pasta, bakery products, sauces and other food products distributed through a portfolio of internationally recognised brands.

In 2024, Barilla reported turnover of approximately €4.883 billion and produced more than two million tonnes of products. Its portfolio included 21 brands and its products were available in more than 100 countries. During the year, the Group allocated approximately €300 million to industrial development, innovation and continuous improvement, including investments related to energy and water resilience, quality, food safety, nutrition and sustainable packaging.

The size and international nature of Barilla’s activities make sustainability particularly relevant to the company. The food industry is closely connected to agricultural production, land use, water availability, energy consumption, packaging, logistics, nutrition and labour conditions across complex supply chains. Many of Barilla’s most significant environmental and social impacts therefore occur not only within its factories but also upstream, particularly in the agricultural production of wheat, tomatoes, basil and other ingredients.

Barilla further redesigned its ESG strategy in 2024. Sustainability is organised around four strategic pillars — Products, People, Value Chain and Climate — and eight strategic programmes covering nutrition and wellbeing, sustainable packaging, employees, health and safety, responsible supply chains, sustainable agriculture, energy and water, and decarbonisation. The framework is intended to translate the company’s sustainability priorities into measurable actions and objectives towards 2030.

The 2024 Sustainability Report also marks a change in Barilla’s reporting approach. The Group incorporated the principle of double materiality and began aligning its sustainability reporting and internal processes with the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) in preparation for requirements introduced by the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD).

## ESG integration

Barilla integrates sustainability considerations into operational processes rather than managing ESG as a separate corporate activity. Environmental and social targets are linked to industrial investments, procurement decisions, agricultural supply chains, product development, human resources, risk management and corporate governance.

The 2024 framework combines quantitative ESG indicators with operational programmes and medium-term targets. Each strategic programme identifies policies, actions, targets and metrics, allowing management to monitor implementation and connect sustainability objectives with the wider corporate strategy.

ESG considerations are also being integrated into Enterprise Risk Management. During 2024, the Global Sustainability Team worked with the Enterprise Risk Management function to analyse ESG risks and opportunities and complete the Group’s Double Materiality Analysis. This allows Barilla to consider both the impacts generated by its activities on people and the environment and the financial risks and opportunities that sustainability issues may create for the company.

## Environmental

### Climate and decarbonisation

Climate change is a significant environmental issue for Barilla because it affects both industrial production and the agricultural supply chains on which the company depends.

In 2024:

- 48% of electricity consumption came from renewable sources.
- Approximately 4 MW of photovoltaic capacity had been installed during the previous five years.
- Overall greenhouse gas emissions were approximately 3.5% lower than the 2022 baseline, according to the sustainability KPI framework.
- New photovoltaic facilities were introduced at production sites, including the Rubbiano sites in Parma.
- Barilla continued investing in energy efficiency and renewable energy generation as part of a broader industrial transition programme.

Barilla’s revised 2030 climate objectives include:

- a 42% reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions compared with 2022;
- a 25% reduction in industrial Scope 3 emissions compared with 2022;
- a 30% reduction in FLAG Scope 3 emissions, covering emissions associated with agriculture, forestry and land use;
- expansion of photovoltaic capacity to 24 MW.

For a food manufacturer, the distinction between industrial Scope 3 emissions and FLAG emissions is important because agricultural raw materials represent a considerable share of value-chain emissions. Barilla’s decarbonisation strategy therefore depends not only on factory efficiency and renewable electricity but also on farming practices, fertiliser use, land management, sourcing and supplier engagement.

In 2024, Barilla renewed its climate commitment within the Science Based Targets initiative framework and submitted more ambitious targets aligned with a 1.5°C pathway. The climate strategy links internal production efficiency with supplier and agricultural initiatives.

### Climate risk management

Barilla assesses climate change as a business and supply-chain risk. Its climate risk analysis covers both transition risks — including regulatory, technological, market and reputational developments — and physical risks, including acute and chronic climate events.

Physical climate risk analysis focuses particularly on:

- strategic agricultural commodities such as durum wheat, soft wheat, rye, tomatoes and basil;
- vulnerability of Barilla manufacturing assets;
- exposure of key supplier manufacturing sites;
- possible effects of extreme weather events on agricultural yields and industrial continuity.

The results are intended to support procurement decisions, diversification of sources and investment in more resilient agricultural practices. Climate-related information is therefore increasingly connected with corporate risk management rather than treated only as environmental reporting.

### Water management

Water is a strategic resource for Barilla both directly, in food manufacturing, and indirectly through agricultural production.

The company pays particular attention to sites located in areas exposed to water stress. In 2024, the volume of water recycled and reused in water-stressed production areas had increased by 164% compared with 2022.

Barilla’s objective for 2030 is to reach a 250% increase in recycled and reused water in water-stressed areas compared with the 2022 baseline.

The strategy includes:

- increased water efficiency in industrial processes;
- recovery and reuse of process water where technically possible;
- monitoring of local water stress;
- prioritisation of investment in facilities located in vulnerable areas;
- technological improvements to reduce water requirements.

This approach recognises that the significance of water use depends not only on total consumption but also on local availability and competing demand for the resource.

### Sustainable packaging and circularity

Packaging is another important part of Barilla’s environmental strategy.

In 2024:

- 99.8% of packaging was designed for recycling globally;
- in Italy, 100% of packaging for pasta, sauces, pesto, bread, biscuits and snacks was designed for recycling;
- more than half of the materials used in packaging came from recycled materials.

Packaging redesign has also been used to reduce material consumption. One example is the removal of the transparent plastic window traditionally used in pasta boxes, reducing the amount of plastic placed on the market. The company has also experimented with higher recycled content in glass packaging.

For 2030, Barilla aims to:

- maintain more than 99% of packaging designed for recycling;
- reach 54% recycled material in packaging;
- save 4,000 tonnes of packaging material compared with 2024 through redesign and eco-design projects.

Barilla therefore treats packaging circularity as both recyclability and material reduction.

## Social

### Employees, diversity and inclusion

Barilla has developed policies addressing equality, inclusion, employee wellbeing and professional development.

The company has maintained global gender pay equality since 2020, based on the principle of equal pay for equal work. The methodology used to assess the gender pay gap was developed internally with external support, including collaboration with KPMG.

In 2024:

- women represented 35% of executive positions;
- Barilla maintained gender pay equality;
- the company’s Internal Reputation Index reached 85%;
- the Group established a target of 40% women in executive positions by 2030.

Barilla also introduced a global parental leave policy providing at least 12 weeks of fully paid parental leave to each parent, independently of gender, marital status or sexual orientation. The policy is intended to support greater equality between caregivers and reduce career disadvantages arising from unequal parental responsibilities.

The company has also developed employee resource groups, diversity and inclusion initiatives and training programmes designed to increase awareness of unconscious bias and foster a more inclusive corporate culture.

### Health and safety

Occupational health and safety is managed through specific performance indicators and a long-term ambition of Zero Accidents.

For 2024, Barilla reported:

- Accident Frequency Index: 8.5;
- Accident Severity Index: 0.29.

The frequency index decreased by approximately 3% compared with the previous year. Barilla has monitored these indicators systematically since 2010 and collects accident, absence and working-hours information from production facilities through a central CSR data-management tool. Health, Safety and Environment managers at production sites are responsible for regular data collection.

The company has established a 2030 frequency-index target of 6.5 while continuing to work towards its broader Zero Accidents ambition.

Barilla’s occupational safety management system is based on UNI EN ISO 45001, providing a structured framework for risk identification, prevention, management review and continuous improvement. The system combines quantitative indicators, a certified management framework and site-level responsibility.

### Nutrition and consumer wellbeing

For a food company, the social dimension of ESG also includes the nutritional characteristics, quality and safety of products.

In 2024:

- 88% of volumes sold contained a maximum of 5 g of sugar per portion;
- 88% did not exceed 0.5 g of salt per portion;
- 90% of volumes sold were a source of fibre;
- 65% of single-portion bakery-product volumes did not exceed 150 kcal per portion.

By 2030, Barilla aims to increase these percentages further, including achieving 95% of volumes sold as a source of fibre.

The company’s quality and food-safety approach also extends upstream to suppliers. Ingredients are assessed according to their specific characteristics and vulnerabilities, while risk analysis, supplier collaboration and increasingly advanced data-analysis systems are used to identify potential food-safety issues before they affect production.

For Barilla, product responsibility therefore includes consumer health, food safety and access to appropriate nutritional information as well as environmental impacts.

### Community engagement

Barilla’s social strategy also includes philanthropy, food donation and corporate volunteering.

During 2024:

- more than €2 million was donated to community initiatives;
- more than 3,700 tonnes of food products were donated;
- more than 1,000 hours of corporate volunteering were undertaken;
- over 300 employees in Italy participated in volunteering initiatives connected particularly with food access and the reduction of food waste.

Activities were undertaken with organisations including food banks, the Italian Red Cross, Caritas and other organisations supporting vulnerable communities.

These initiatives have a direct connection with Barilla’s core business. Food donations and actions addressing food accessibility allow the company to use its products, logistics capabilities, employee engagement and partnerships to respond to social needs associated with food insecurity.

## Governance

### ESG governance

Barilla has developed a structured ESG governance model involving the ESG Board, the Global Sustainability Team and a network of ESG Leaders across different business functions. More than 150 employees contribute to the implementation and monitoring of sustainability initiatives.

The ESG Board provides strategic direction, while the Global Sustainability Team coordinates activities, stakeholder engagement and performance monitoring. ESG Leaders support implementation within individual business areas. Sustainability strategies and priorities are ultimately reviewed by senior management and the Board of Directors.

This structure is intended to integrate ESG considerations into business decisions rather than treat sustainability as a separate corporate function.

### Double materiality and risk management

In 2024, Barilla strengthened the integration of ESG issues into its Enterprise Risk Management system and carried out a Double Materiality Analysis.

The assessment considers both:

- the environmental and social impacts generated by Barilla and its value chain;
- the financial risks and opportunities that ESG issues may create for the company.

Key risks include climate change, product quality and safety, workforce conditions, supply-chain disruption, availability of agricultural raw materials and regulatory developments.

### Sustainable agriculture and responsible sourcing

Agricultural sourcing is one of Barilla’s main ESG priorities. In 2024, approximately 815,000 tonnes of raw materials were sourced according to Barilla Sustainable Farming specifications, involving more than 7,000 farmers.

The company applies dedicated standards for key agricultural supply chains and is increasingly promoting regenerative agriculture. Its target is to source 250,000 tonnes of raw materials annually through regenerative agriculture by 2030.

Responsible sourcing is supported by the Sustainable Agriculture Code, supplier requirements, animal-welfare standards and risk-based assessments covering environmental, social and biodiversity issues.

Barilla also achieved 100% cage-free eggs in 2024.

Digital traceability solutions are increasingly used to improve supply-chain transparency, including blockchain applications in the basil supply chain.

## Ratings and evidence

Barilla uses a strategic KPI system to monitor progress towards its 2030 sustainability objectives.

The company’s Internal Reputation Index reached 85% in 2024, based on RepTrak methodology.

Externally, Barilla ranked 29th in the 2024 Global RepTrak 100 and was the highest-ranked food company in the ranking.

Its ESG monitoring system includes indicators related to:

- renewable energy and greenhouse gas emissions;
- water reuse;
- sustainable packaging;
- occupational health and safety;
- gender equality;
- sustainable agricultural sourcing;
- supply-chain performance.

These indicators allow the company to compare current results with medium-term targets.

## Regulatory compliance

Barilla is progressively adapting its ESG governance and reporting systems to the evolving European regulatory framework.

The company has started aligning its sustainability reporting with the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS).

Key developments include:

- implementation of Double Materiality Analysis;
- stronger integration of ESG into risk management;
- improved governance of sustainability information;
- preparation for more structured value-chain due diligence;
- climate targets aligned with the Science Based Targets initiative;
- use of independently verified Environmental Product Declarations.

Barilla has also identified the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) as an important reference for strengthening future supplier and value-chain assessments.

Overall, the company is moving from mainly voluntary sustainability practices towards a more structured system based on traceability, risk assessment and regulatory compliance.

## Recommendations

The source case study identifies several areas where Barilla could strengthen its approach:

- **ESG data and ESRS alignment:** improve documentation supporting key ESG indicators, including data ownership, calculation methods, reporting boundaries and verification procedures.
- **Scope 3 emissions:** increase the use of verified supplier and farm-level data to improve measurement accuracy and support progress towards climate targets.
- **Regenerative agriculture:** publish clearer annual milestones covering tonnes sourced, farmers involved, hectares covered and measurable environmental results.
- **Supply-chain due diligence:** extend risk assessment progressively beyond direct suppliers and strengthen monitoring of human-rights, biodiversity, climate and social risks.
- **External assurance:** expand independent verification to additional ESG indicators, particularly greenhouse gas emissions, water, sustainable agriculture and supply-chain performance.
