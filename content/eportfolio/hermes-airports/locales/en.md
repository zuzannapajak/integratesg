## Company Overview

**Name:** Hermes Airports (Operator of Larnaca and Pafos Airports)

**Sector:** Transportation & Infrastructure / Aviation (Airport Operations)

**Location:** Larnaca International Airport, 6650 Larnaca, Cyprus (Registered Headquarters)

**Size:** Over 13.75 million annual passengers managed across 2 international airports, supports ~25,000 direct and indirect jobs across the airport ecosystem, operates under a 25-year Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) concession agreement

**Reporting Year:** 2025 (5th Annual Sustainability Report covering the period 01 January 2025 to 31 December 2025)

Hermes Airports Ltd is an international consortium comprising nine Cypriot and foreign shareholders responsible for managing and operating Cyprus’s two commercial aviation gateways: Larnaka International Airport and Pafos International Airport. Assuming management under a Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) concession agreement with the Republic of Cyprus, the company drives key regional connectivity, tourism infrastructure, and trade activities across the island. Hermes Airports is a pioneer in sustainable aviation management within the Mediterranean, maintaining carbon neutrality (ACI Level 4+ Transition) and running major renewable energy and decarbonization projects across its airport facilities.

## ESG practices integrated and implementation methods

### Environmental Practices

Hermes Airports embeds environmental stewardship into its core business strategy for both Larnaka and Pafos International Airports, adhering to structured governance and international reporting frameworks like the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI). Under its corporate Energy Policy, the operator holds the ISO 50001:2011 certification for Energy Management. The company monitors, measures, and transparently reports its environmental performance to ensure continuous alignment with global aviation standards and sustainability best practices.

A central pillar of the airports' environmental strategy is carbon management and the transition toward renewable energy. Both Larnaka and Pafos International Airports achieved carbon neutrality in 2019 and have earned the prestigious ACI Level 4+ Transition accreditation. Hermes Airports has pledged to achieve net-zero carbon emissions for operations under its direct control by 2050. To drive this decarbonization trajectory, the operator constructed major solar photovoltaic plants—a 3.5 MWp installation at Larnaka and a 1.1 MWp installation at Pafos—which collectively generate enough clean electricity to supply roughly 28% of the airports' total energy demands.

Beyond internal operational management, Hermes Airports actively participates in ecosystem protection and environmental awareness initiatives within the local community. The operator collaborates directly with Cyprus's Department of Environment to help protect key local habitats, such as the environmentally sensitive Larnaka Salt Lake. Through targeted educational and outreach campaigns, Hermes Airports informs incoming passengers and tourists about the ecological significance of nearby wetlands and biodiversity, extending its environmental commitments beyond airport infrastructure.

### Social Practices

Achieved an Employee Satisfaction Score of 4.45/5 in 2024, the highest in its history, alongside maintaining zero work-related injuries or ill-health cases. Social responsibility forms an integral part of Hermes Airports' core operating model, driving its efforts to support surrounding communities, care for its workforce, and enhance the overall passenger experience. Through a structured corporate social responsibility framework, the airport operator prioritizes respect for people, active community development, and local cultural engagement. Key public-facing social initiatives include partnerships with non-governmental organizations, charity donations to local schools and welfare foundations, community art exhibitions, and joint public awareness campaigns – such as collaborative anti-human trafficking programs conducted alongside national ministries and police authorities.

Internally, Hermes Airports focuses heavily on personnel safety, employee well-being, and workplace development. The company enforces robust Health, Safety, and Environment (HSE) protocols compliant with national regulatory standards to ensure a safe work environment for staff, contractors, visitors, and third parties across Larnaka and Pafos airports. Furthermore, the operator invests in continuous personal and professional development for its workforce, promotes diversity and inclusion, and has earned international recognition, such as Gold accreditation from Investors in People, for its employee well-being practices.

Aviation safety and passenger-centric operational standards complete the company's social responsibility pillars. Hermes Airports systematically implements, maintains, and updates safety procedures to guarantee high levels of security and operational integrity across all aviation activities under its control. Concurrently, the operator enhances passenger experience by maintaining high-quality service standards, providing specialized accessibility support for persons with reduced mobility and neurodivergent travelers, and embedding a distinct sense of local Cypriot culture and hospitality within the terminal environments.

### Governance Practices

Actively participates in the EU-funded TULIPS project, focusing on "Circular Airports" and "Zero Emission Airside Operations" to align with regional aviation decarbonization targets. The TULIPS project is an EU-funded initiative under the Horizon 2020 research and innovation program aimed at accelerating the transition toward low-carbon mobility and highly sustainable airport operations. Led by Amsterdam Airport Schiphol, the consortium brings together 29 international partners – including airport operators, airlines, research institutes, and technology companies – to develop, test, and deploy cutting-edge green technologies across the aviation sector. Supported by €25 million in European Union funding, the project's ultimate target is to enable zero-emission and zero-waste airports by 2030, contributing to the broader goal of achieving climate-neutral European aviation by 2050.

At the core of the project are 17 practical demonstrator initiatives hosted primarily at Amsterdam Airport Schiphol as the main testing ground, with partner airports in Oslo, Turin, and Larnaca selected to implement and replicate key solutions. These initiatives span critical sustainability areas, including the integration of Sustainable Aviation Fuels (SAF), the deployment of hydrogen technology and liquid hydrogen infrastructure, the electrification of airport ground handling equipment, and the implementation of circular economy practices. By evaluating these innovative solutions in real-world operational environments and sharing findings across the partner network, TULIPS aims to create scalable, standardized benchmarks that can be rolled out across airports worldwide.

## Information on indices scoring

Reports under the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) and the Airport Carbon Accreditation (ACA) framework, currently working toward Level 4+ (Transition/Transformation).

## Success in regulatory compliance

Achieved a 75.58% decrease in Scope 1 emissions at Pafos Airport and a 46% reduction in emissions from refrigerant gases at Larnaca Airport in 2024.

## Recommendations for improvement

Focus on expanding PV capacity to reach 100% renewable energy coverage for daytime operations to further mitigate rising energy costs and carbon exposure.
