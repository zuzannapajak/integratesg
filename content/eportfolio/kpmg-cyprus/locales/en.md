## Company Overview

**Name:** KPMG Limited (Cyprus)

**Sector:** Professional Services / Accounting, Audit, Tax & Advisory

**Location:** 14 Esperidon Street, 1087 Nicosia, Cyprus (Headquarters; operating across 6 offices in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaka, Pafos, Paralimni, and Polis Chrysochous)

**Size:** Over 730 professionals and staff across Cyprus, Part of the global KPMG network operating across 138+ countries

**Reporting Year:** 2025 (Transparency Report 2025 for the financial year ended 31 December 2025)

Operating in Cyprus since 1948, KPMG Cyprus is one of the leading professional services firms on the island, providing a full suite of audit, tax, legal, and advisory solutions to domestic and international clients. As a member firm of the global KPMG organization, it combines deep local market expertise with international methodologies to serve corporations, financial institutions, and public sector bodies. The firm actively drives digital transformation, audit quality enhancement, and sustainability advisory, assisting organizations in implementing ESG strategies, CSRD compliance, and robust risk governance structures.

## ESG practices integrated and implementation methods

### Environmental Practices

Operates two certified "Green Offices" in Cyprus and utilizes the Synesgy platform (as of February 2025) to track and minimize the environmental footprint of its consultancy operations. The Synesgy Cyprus portal serves as a dedicated digital entryway for local businesses, financial institutions, and supply chain partners to access CRIF’s global Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) assessment platform. Operating in compliance with local regulations and international reporting frameworks—such as the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI), the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), and European Taxonomy standards—the localized platform enables Cypriot companies to complete tailored ESG self-assessments, benchmark their operational performance, and manage sustainability disclosures within a unified ecosystem. Through the platform, organizations can measure their ESG impact, upload supporting evidence for automated and manual data quality checks, and receive an official ESG score alongside a downloadable 12-month certificate and detailed action plan. For supply chain managers, commercial buyers, and financial institutions operating in Cyprus, the service provides specialized analytics dashboards to monitor supplier sustainability performance, streamline regulatory compliance, mitigate reputational risks, and foster sustainable business practices across regional and global value chains.

KPMG Cyprus outlines its commitment to environmental stewardship and internal sustainability in its Transparency Report 2025 through the global framework of Our Impact Plan (specifically focusing on the "Planet" pillar). The report highlights the firm's strategic initiatives to reduce its direct operational carbon footprint, aiming for net-zero carbon emissions by 2030. To achieve this, the firm focuses on minimizing energy consumption across its office premises, implementing energy-efficiency solutions, transitioning to renewable energy sources, and reducing business travel and paper waste across its operations.

Beyond direct operations, the report emphasizes embedding environmental considerations across KPMG's broader value chain and client engagement activities. KPMG Cyprus actively evaluates Scope 1, Scope 2, and material Scope 3 emissions while engaging with local and international suppliers to encourage sustainable procurement practices. Furthermore, the firm integrates environmental risk assessments into its audit methodologies, assurance services, and corporate advisory offerings, ensuring that climate-related risks and reporting standards (such as CSRD and EU Taxonomy) are transparently disclosed and aligned with global public interest responsibilities.

### Social Practices

Prioritizes lifelong learning, with an average of 500 people trained annually in ethics, anti-bribery, and data privacy to ensure a highly skilled and socially responsible workforce. In its Transparency Report 2025, KPMG Cyprus highlights its internal social practices through the "People" pillar of its global Our Impact Plan, placing workforce well-being, inclusion, and continuous professional development at the center of its operating model. Across its six offices in Cyprus, the firm focuses on creating an inclusive, purpose-led culture where diversity is respected and equal growth opportunities are provided to its 730 professionals. The report underscores ongoing investments in employee health and well-being programs, structured learning paths, and specialized upskilling initiatives – particularly around responsible AI usage, digital analytics, and ESG frameworks – to foster talent retention and support career progression.

Beyond internal workforce practices, the report details KPMG Cyprus's broader social commitments to community impact, public trust, and social responsibility. Guided by its core values – Integrity, Excellence, Courage, Together, and For Better – the firm aligns its social agenda with community development, supporting local educational, social welfare, and volunteer initiatives. Additionally, KPMG links corporate quality management directly to public interest, enforcing fair performance evaluations and transparent career pathways while empowering its professionals to drive positive social change across the broader Cypriot community.

### Governance Practices

Publishes an annual Transparency Report, outlining its whistleblowing mechanisms, data security protocols, and independence assessments in audit and advisory services. KPMG Cyprus details a robust corporate governance framework anchored by strict ethical standards, clear organizational oversight, and absolute independence. Operating under KPMG International governance guidelines and local Cypriot legal structures, the firm's leadership team enforces a comprehensive System of Quality Management (SoQM) aligned with International Standard on Quality Management 1 (ISQM 1). This framework outlines clear lines of accountability, risk assessment procedures, and internal control mechanisms to ensure compliance with regulatory expectations and protect the integrity of financial reporting across the capital markets.

A major focus of the governance practices highlighted in the 2025 report is the ethical deployment of technology and continuous audit quality control. The firm maintains strict policies on partner rotation, conflict management, anti-money laundering (AML), and independence monitoring across all assurance and advisory engagements. Furthermore, KPMG Cyprus integrates risk governance into its digital transformation strategy – enforcing clear compliance protocols, cybersecurity controls, and ethical boundaries for teams utilizing data analytics, the cloud-based KPMG Clara audit platform, and artificial intelligence tools.

## Success in regulatory compliance

Adheres to the Omnibus Framework and the CSRD (Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive), serving as a benchmark for the very clients they advise. The Sustainability Regulatory Radar (January 2026, Issue 19) highlights a significant tightening of supervisor expectations regarding environmental and climate-related risk management for financial institutions. In the UK, the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) issued Supervisory Statement SS5/25, replacing SS3/19 to mandate that banks and insurers more deeply embed climate risk into strategic decision-making, board oversight, scenario analysis, and stress testing. Meanwhile, the European Central Bank (ECB) reaffirmed its focus on monitoring financial institutions' transition plans to a green economy, their resilience against the physical impacts of climate change, and the growing financial risks associated with nature and biodiversity loss.

European and global regulators are restructuring sustainability disclosures and market frameworks to emphasize material climate outcomes. Amendments to the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) recalibrated reporting obligations, limiting mandatory compliance to larger corporations while EFRAG revised standards to simplify double materiality assessments, particularly for greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions. Additionally, new regulatory frameworks for ESG ratings and benchmarks are advancing in both the UK and EU to enhance market transparency and prevent greenwashing, while emerging research explores how artificial intelligence can be leveraged to optimize climate outcomes and accelerate the broader energy transition.
