## Company overview

**Name:** Powszechny Zakład Ubezpieczeń S.A. (PZU S.A.)

**Sector:** Insurance and Financial Services

**Location:** Warsaw, Poland

**Size:** Poland's largest insurance group, market leader in non-life and life insurance

**Reporting Year:** 2023

Powszechny Zakład Ubezpieczeń S.A. (PZU S.A.) is Poland’s largest and oldest insurance company, founded in 1803 and headquartered in Warsaw. As a publicly traded company listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange (WSE: PZU), it is a key component of the WIG30 index. PZU Group offers nearly 200 insurance products, covering both life and non-life insurance and has expanded into banking, investment and healthcare services. The company has played a significant role in the Polish financial sector, including major acquisitions such as LINK4 and a stake in Bank Pekao.

## Recommendations

Scope 3 emissions strategy: while PZU has achieved climate neutrality for Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 2024, the company should develop a comprehensive strategy to measure, disclose and reduce Scope 3 financed emissions from its investment and underwriting portfolios, establishing science-based targets validated by SBTi with clear interim milestones for 2030 and 2040. This would align with emerging regulatory expectations and investor demands for full value chain decarbonisation.

Enhanced ESG product innovation: PZU should accelerate development of innovative insurance products that incentivize policyholder ESG behaviour, such as usage-based insurance with discounts for electric vehicles, parametric insurance for climate adaptation projects and biodiversity-linked insurance products that reward ecosystem restoration. Additionally, creating ESG-linked investment products for retail customers would democratize sustainable investing and strengthen market leadership.

Digital ESG transparency platform: the company should develop a customer-facing digital platform that provides real-time ESG impact reporting, allowing policyholders and investors to track how their premiums and investments contribute to environmental and social outcomes, with transparent metrics on carbon avoided, renewable energy financed and social impact generated. This would differentiate PZU in customer experience and strengthen stakeholder engagement.

Supplier decarbonisation program: beyond the current 100% ESG procurement integration, PZU should establish a structured supplier decarbonisation program with technical assistance, financing options and preferential contract terms for suppliers that achieve verified emissions reductions, creating a multiplier effect across the value chain. This would address indirect emissions while strengthening supply chain resilience and demonstrating industry leadership in Scope 3 management.
