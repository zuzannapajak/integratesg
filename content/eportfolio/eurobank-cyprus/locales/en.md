## Company Overview

**Name:** Eurobank Cyprus Ltd.

**Sector:** Financials (Financial Services) / Banking

**Location:** 200 Lemesou & Athalassas Avenue, 2025 Strovolos, Nicosia, Cyprus (Headquarters)

**Size:** Employees: ~3,300+ professionals (Combined Group post-merger with Hellenic Bank), Extensive network of banking centers and local branches across Cyprus

**Reporting Year:** 2025 (Annual Financial Report for the year ended 31 December 2025)

Eurobank Cyprus (operating under the Eurobank Group) is a leading financial institution in Cyprus, specializing in wholesale banking, corporate and investment banking, wealth management, private banking, and international business banking. Following its landmark strategic merger with Hellenic Bank, Eurobank established one of the largest combined banking forces in Cyprus, significantly expanding its footprint across retail and commercial markets. The bank is recognized for its strong capital base, high operational efficiency, and commitment to driving digital transformation and sustainable finance across the region.

## ESG practices integrated and implementation methods

### Environmental Practices

Eurobank Cyprus’s Sustainability Annual Reports repository serves as the central hub for the bank's non-financial disclosures, tracking its progression toward integrating Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) principles across all business activities. The page outlines the institution's strategic alignment with international sustainability benchmarks, most notably the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the UNEP FI Principles for Responsible Banking. By maintaining a publicly accessible archive of annual reports spanning consecutive years, Eurobank provides transparent accountability regarding its performance targets, ESG disclosures, and long-term commitment to combining sound financial performance with corporate environmental and social responsibility.

The strategic framework reflected throughout the reporting suite is built upon two distinct levels of impact: operational impact and financed impact. On the operational front, the bank documents concrete milestones and action plans designed to minimize its direct carbon footprint, accelerate digital transformation towards a paperless branch network, optimize resource and water efficiency, and establish sustainable procurement protocols. Simultaneously, the financed impact pillar evaluates the environmental and social consequences of the bank’s lending and investment portfolios, detailing metrics on green loan disbursements, financed greenhouse gas emissions, and decarbonization trajectories targeting net-zero operations by 2050.

In addition to environmental indicators, the reports highlight Eurobank's internal governance structure and social responsibility initiatives. The disclosures outline commitments to fostering a diverse and inclusive workplace, eliminating gender pay gaps, enhancing employee wellbeing, and promoting ESG literacy among staff and clients. By incorporating ESG and climate-related risk assessments directly into its core Risk Management Framework and offering dedicated green retail and commercial financing products, the published annual sustainability reports demonstrate how Eurobank systematically embeds responsible banking practices into its broader corporate growth strategy.

### Social Practices

Eurobank’s social practices prioritize internal human capital development, workplace equity, and employee well-being. A central pillar of these human resources initiatives is continuous learning and professional growth, with 98.85% of Group employees actively participating in structured training programs. The group has strengthened its commitment to workplace equality by endorsing the UN Women’s Empowerment Principles (WEPs), maintaining gender equity in workforce representation, and continuously monitoring and reducing the gender pay gap. Additionally, Eurobank focuses on youth attraction and talent retention through dedicated initiatives such as the TechYourFuture program in collaboration with Microsoft, the BeAPro graduate talent program, and active participation in international "Brain Regain" career events to recruit Greek professionals working abroad.

Customer-centricity, accessibility, and high operational safety form the second major component of Eurobank's social agenda. To ensure inclusive banking, 100% of the bank's ATM network has been adapted to meet full accessibility requirements for persons with disabilities. The institution emphasizes customer trust and consumer protection, maintaining strong data privacy and cybersecurity measures with zero reported personal data breaches or regulatory incidents. Eurobank maintains open communication channels through extensive customer feedback analysis, successfully resolving 97.2% of customer complaints and driving digital channel usage to account for 96% of all non-cash transaction volumes.

Community engagement, social impact funding, and regional relief projects complete Eurobank’s social framework. The bank invests heavily in youth empowerment and education through the long-standing Moving Education Forward program, which has awarded over 23,500 high-achieving high school students, alongside demographic initiatives like Moving Family Forward supporting families in local communities. Beyond education, Eurobank fosters entrepreneurial innovation through its egg (enter-grow-go) business accelerator, into which it has invested €13 million since launch. Eurobank also delivers immediate humanitarian and infrastructure aid, allocating €12.5 million toward flood reconstruction efforts in Thessaly and €25 million to the national school renovation program, while mobilizing employee volunteer initiatives to support local welfare projects.

### Governance Practices

Eurobank’s corporate governance framework is designed to align with strict European and national regulatory standards, establishing clear supervisory structures to drive transparency, ethical accountability, and long-term value creation. Governance oversight is anchored by the Board of Directors and its specialized committees—including the Audit, Risk, Nomination, Remuneration, and Sustainability Committees—which collectively oversee strategic decision-making, financial reporting integrity, internal controls, and corporate compliance. The Board enforces a robust code of conduct, rigorous conflict-of-interest policies, anti-money laundering (AML) controls, and zero-tolerance mandates for bribery and corruption across all domestic and international operations.

A central component of Eurobank's governance is its comprehensive approach to enterprise risk management and climate-related governance. The Group systematically integrates Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) considerations and climate risks into its core risk management framework, credit approval protocols, and capital planning processes. Specialized executive-level bodies, such as the Sustainability Committee and dedicated Risk Management Divisions, monitor physical and transition climate risks, oversee compliance with European sustainability disclosures, and ensure that the Bank's strategic transformation alignment remains resilient against evolving macroeconomic and environmental challenges.

Information technology governance, cyber resilience, and data protection complete Eurobank’s governance model. Under its strategic transformation program, the Group enforces stringent IT security architectures, cloud migration governance, and data governance frameworks to ensure operational continuity and protect client data integrity. By adhering to international privacy guidelines (such as GDPR) and executing regular internal and independent external audits across its operational, compliance, and technology units, Eurobank ensures continuous governance oversight, regulatory alignment, and stakeholder trust.
