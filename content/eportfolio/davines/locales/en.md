## Company overview

**Name:** Davines Group

**Sector:** Professional Cosmetics / Personal Care Products

**Location:** Parma, Italy

**Size:** International company with more than 1,000 employees worldwide and operations in more than 90 countries

**Reporting Year:** 2024

Davines Group is an Italian family-owned company founded in Parma in 1983 as a research laboratory. Today, the Group operates internationally in the professional haircare and skincare markets through the Davines and [comfort zone] brands. Its business model combines economic growth with a strong focus on people, communities and environmental sustainability.

Sustainability is not treated as a separate corporate activity but is incorporated into the Group's overall business philosophy and long-term development model. Davines describes its approach as increasingly regenerative, with the objective of moving beyond simply reducing negative impacts and contributing positively to people, communities and ecosystems. The Group's sustainability vision is structured around three main areas:

- Regenerating our planet, with a focus on decarbonisation, circularity, biodiversity and water.
- Supporting our collaborators and community, through employee wellbeing, inclusion, development and community engagement.
- B Responsible, integrating ethics, transparency, stakeholder governance and responsible business practices.
- Davines became a Certified B Corporation in 2016 and its Italian and US companies adopted the legal status of Benefit Corporation in 2019. This legal and organisational structure formally incorporates social and environmental objectives into the company's governance alongside economic objectives.
- The company publishes an annual Sustainability Report to communicate progress, objectives and areas for improvement. The 2024 report presents the Group's results and its sustainability objectives towards 2030.

## ESG integration

Davines Group's ESG approach is based on the principle that sustainability should be integrated into products, production processes, supply chains, corporate governance and stakeholder relationships.

### The implementation approach combines

- Long-term, measurable sustainability targets.
- Science-based climate targets.
- Product and packaging redesign.
- Responsible sourcing of raw materials.
- Biodiversity and regenerative agriculture initiatives.
- Employee engagement and development.
- Benefit Corporation governance.
- B Corp assessment and periodic recertification.
- Annual sustainability reporting and stakeholder transparency.
- The company's approach is therefore characterised by a combination of internal targets, external assessment, operational initiatives and continuous improvement.

## Environmental

### Climate strategy and decarbonisation

- Climate action represents one of the main environmental priorities of Davines Group.
- The Group joined the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) in 2022, strengthening its commitment to reducing greenhouse gas emissions through science-based targets.
- The company's climate strategy includes:
- Science-based targets for the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions.
- Reduction of absolute Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 42% by 2030, compared with the 2022 baseline.
- Reduction of Scope 3 emissions per kilogram of bulk product by 51.6% by 2030.
- Long-term commitment to achieving Net Zero emissions by 2050.
- Monitoring of emissions across the value chain, recognising the importance of indirect emissions associated with suppliers, materials, transportation and products.
- This approach is particularly relevant for the cosmetics sector, where a significant proportion of environmental impact can occur outside the company's direct operations.

### Environmental priorities

- Davines identifies four strategic environmental areas:
- Decarbonisation – reducing greenhouse gas emissions throughout the value chain.
- Circularity – reducing the use of virgin materials and promoting reuse and recycling.
- Biodiversity – protecting ecosystems and encouraging more sustainable sourcing practices.
- Water – reducing water consumption and improving water management.
- These priorities are integrated into the company's longer-term sustainability objectives rather than being implemented as isolated projects.

### Circular economy and packaging

- Packaging represents a particularly important environmental issue for a cosmetics company.
- Davines has developed a dedicated Packaging Research Charter, originally introduced in 2011 and updated in 2023. The Charter establishes eco-design principles aimed at improving the environmental performance of packaging throughout its life cycle.
- Implementation methods include:
- Reducing the quantity of materials used in packaging.
- Increasing the use of recycled materials.
- Improving recyclability and reuse.
- Reducing dependence on virgin fossil-based plastics.
- Applying life-cycle thinking to packaging design.
- Integrating environmental considerations into product development.
- The company's circularity strategy therefore moves away from the traditional linear model of "take, use and dispose" towards a model based on reducing, reusing and recycling materials.

### Biodiversity and regenerative agriculture

- Biodiversity is another central element of Davines Group's environmental strategy.
- The Group promotes responsible sourcing practices and seeks to protect biodiversity through the selection of raw materials and collaboration with agricultural producers.
- A notable initiative is the European Regenerative Organic Centre (EROC), established in Parma on a 17-hectare site in partnership with the Rodale Institute. The centre focuses on research and training related to regenerative organic agriculture and the development of healthier soils and sustainable agricultural practices.
- Davines has also developed partnerships aimed at protecting agricultural biodiversity. Since 2014, for example, its haircare division has collaborated with Slow Food, incorporating active ingredients from Italian Slow Food Presidia into selected products and supporting farming communities.
- Implementation methods include:
- Responsible sourcing of natural ingredients.
- Promotion of regenerative agricultural practices.
- Partnerships with organisations supporting biodiversity.
- Research and training on regenerative agriculture.
- Integration of biodiversity considerations into product formulation and supply chains.

### Water management

- Water is identified as one of the four key environmental priorities of the Group.
- Davines' approach focuses on:
- Reducing water consumption in production facilities.
- Improving water-use efficiency.
- Monitoring the water footprint of operations.
- Developing water-reduction targets for production and offices.
- Exploring water recovery and reuse solutions.
- The Group's 2030 objectives include reducing total water consumption at the Davines Group Village and significantly reducing water-use intensity during production.
- This approach links operational efficiency with environmental protection, particularly in relation to the use and treatment of water within manufacturing activities.

## Governance

### Benefit Corporation

- A central element of Davines Group's governance model is the adoption of the Benefit Corporation legal status.
- The Italian and US companies of the Group became Benefit Corporations in 2019. This change formally incorporated common-benefit objectives into the company's legal and governance framework, alongside the pursuit of profit.
- The model means that environmental and social considerations are incorporated into corporate decision-making and are not treated simply as voluntary corporate social responsibility activities.
- Implementation methods include:
- Integration of common-benefit objectives into the company statute.
- Consideration of stakeholders in strategic decisions.
- Annual reporting on impact.
- Accountability for social and environmental objectives.
- Alignment between corporate purpose and sustainability strategy.

### B Corporation Certification

- Davines has been a Certified B Corporation since 2016.
- The B Corp certification evaluates companies through the B Impact Assessment (BIA), covering areas related to social and environmental performance, accountability and transparency.
- Davines obtained a B Impact Score of:
- 99 points in its first certification in 2016.
- 117.4 points in the 2020 recertification.
- 123.5 points in the 2023 recertification.
- The increase in the score demonstrates the company's progress through successive assessment cycles and its use of external assessment as a mechanism for continuous improvement.
- The B Corp model provides an external framework for assessing the company's performance and encourages the integration of sustainability across different areas of the organisation.

### Stakeholder governance

- Davines identifies itself as a stakeholder-driven company, meaning that decision-making is intended to consider the interests of a broader group of stakeholders rather than shareholders alone.
- Relevant stakeholders include:
- Employees.
- Customers.
- Suppliers.
- Business partners.
- Local communities.
- Environmental stakeholders.
- Civil society organisations.
- This approach supports the company's Benefit Corporation status and broader objective of creating and redistributing value among different stakeholder groups.

### Transparency and reporting

- Davines publishes an annual Sustainability Report to communicate its environmental, social and governance performance.
- The reporting process provides information on:
- Sustainability objectives.
- Environmental performance.
- Social initiatives.
- Governance practices.
- Progress towards long-term targets.
- Areas requiring further improvement.
- The company describes the Sustainability Report as an accountability and transparency tool, enabling stakeholders to follow the evolution of its sustainability journey.
- The company also produces impact reporting associated with its Benefit Corporation status, further strengthening the link between corporate purpose, governance and measurable impact.

## Recommendations

Despite the strong integration of ESG principles, several areas could be further strengthened.

Improved Scope 3 data quality

Davines should continue increasing the use of primary data from suppliers and other value-chain actors to improve the accuracy of Scope 3 emissions measurement.

This would strengthen the reliability and verifiability of the company's climate indicators, particularly in areas where emissions depend heavily on suppliers, raw materials, packaging and logistics.

Stronger ESG data verification

The company could further strengthen external assurance and verification of selected ESG indicators.

### Structured evidence packs for key indicators could improve

- Traceability of ESG data.
- Audit readiness.
- Comparability between reporting years.
- Stakeholder confidence.
- Alignment with emerging European reporting expectations.
  Further integration with ESRS

Davines could continue strengthening the mapping between its existing sustainability indicators and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS).

This would help ensure that existing voluntary sustainability reporting can progressively support more structured European sustainability disclosure requirements where applicable.

Supplier ESG due diligence

Given the importance of Scope 3 emissions and responsible sourcing, Davines could further develop systematic ESG assessment of key suppliers. Potential measures include:

- ESG questionnaires and scorecards.
- Supplier sustainability audits.
- Environmental and social improvement plans.
- Monitoring of high-risk suppliers.
- Increased use of primary environmental data.
  Circular economy and packaging

Davines could continue increasing the proportion of recycled and recyclable materials and reducing dependence on virgin fossil-based plastics. Future improvements could include:

- Further packaging lightweighting.
- Increased recycled content.
- Greater use of refill and reuse models where technically feasible.
- More systematic measurement of packaging circularity.
- Life-cycle assessment of major packaging categories.
  Biodiversity performance indicators

The Group could further develop quantitative indicators measuring the outcomes of its biodiversity and regenerative agriculture initiatives. This could include:

- Number and percentage of sustainably sourced ingredients.
- Land under regenerative management.
- Biodiversity-related supplier criteria.
- Soil health indicators.
- Positive biodiversity impacts associated with sourcing initiatives.
  Stakeholder engagement

Davines could further formalise stakeholder engagement by developing structured mechanisms for gathering and responding to stakeholder feedback. This could include:

- Regular stakeholder consultations.
- Supplier sustainability forums.
- Employee sustainability surveys.
- Community feedback mechanisms.
- Transparent reporting on how stakeholder input influences ESG decisions.

### Overall assessment

Davines Group represents a strong example of an organisation integrating ESG principles into its business model, governance structure, products, supply chain and long-term strategy. Its main strengths are:

- Science-based climate targets.
- A clear focus on decarbonisation, circularity, biodiversity and water.
- Benefit Corporation legal status.
- B Corp certification and periodic external assessment.
- Transparent annual sustainability reporting.
- Investment in regenerative agriculture and biodiversity.
- Integration of social sustainability into employee and community initiatives.
- Long-term commitment to continuous improvement.
  The combination of external certification, measurable targets, stakeholder governance and operational sustainability initiatives makes Davines a useful case for understanding how ESG can be integrated into a company beyond isolated environmental or social projects. At the same time, further progress could be achieved through stronger Scope 3 data, enhanced supplier due diligence, more detailed biodiversity indicators, increased external verification and closer alignment of ESG metrics with European sustainability reporting frameworks.
