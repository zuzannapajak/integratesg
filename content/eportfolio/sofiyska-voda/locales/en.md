## Company overview

**Name:** Sofiyska Voda AD (Sofia Water), part of the Veolia Group

**Sector:** Water supply and wastewater services

**Location:** Sofia, Bulgaria

**Size:** Bulgaria's largest water utility, serving approximately 1.26–1.4 million residents; ownership split between Veolia (77.1%) and Sofia Municipality (22.9%)

**Reporting Year:** 2023–2024 (concession originally awarded 2000; Veolia majority shareholder since 2010)

Sofiyska Voda operates Bulgaria's only water public-private partnership, under a 25-year concession contract with Sofia Municipality originally awarded in 2000 and renegotiated in 2018. Since Veolia took over the majority shareholding in 2010, the company has invested approximately €225 million in network rehabilitation and modernisation (through 2019), and has proposed a further €324 million in infrastructure upgrades through 2034 contingent on concession extension. As the second-largest investor in the Bulgarian water sector after the state since 2017, Sofiyska Voda occupies a structurally important position in the country's water and wastewater infrastructure.

## ESG integration

ESG integration at Sofiyska Voda is embedded in its core concession obligations, infrastructure investment programme, and stakeholder governance — reflecting both Veolia Group standards and the specific accountability structure of operating a municipal concession.

## Environmental

### Water loss reduction and network modernisation

- Total water losses reduced from 122 million m³ (2010) to 59 million m³ (2019)
- Installation of pressure-reducing valves (PRVs) cut non-revenue water by 17% in the programme's early phase
- 580 km of drinking water network rehabilitated or newly constructed since 2010

### Energy efficiency and emissions

- The Kubratovo wastewater treatment plant — one of the most energy-efficient in Europe — converts biogas from treatment into electricity and heat, saving an estimated 70,000 tonnes of CO₂ per year
- Kubratovo holds Gold Standard certification for emissions reduction, with further reduction plans underway

### Digital and technological environmental management

- Upgrade to Esri's ArcGIS Utility Network for water and sewer network management, improving modelling, data quality and operational efficiency
- First water utility in the Balkans to deploy digital algae-bloom monitoring (LG Sonic) at the Iskar Reservoir, the main source of Sofia's drinking water
- Sludge and other treatment by-products managed in line with environmental standards across the service lifecycle

## Social

### Employee and workforce practices

- Regular health and safety training, risk assessments and incident monitoring embedded in daily operations for employees and contractors

### Customer and community engagement

- Customer satisfaction reached 87% in 2019, supported by growing use of digital communication channels
- Active participation in public forums on climate and flood risk (e.g., with WWF Bulgaria and Sofia Municipality's urban planning agency Sofiaplan), reflecting a broader view of water management as a social and climate-adaptation responsibility

### Affordability and continuity of service

- The 2018 concession renegotiation locked in a steady investment flow (~€20 million/year) to Sofia Municipality while protecting service continuity and pricing

## Governance

### Ownership and oversight structure

- Public-private governance model: 77.1% Veolia / 22.9% Sofia Municipality — the only such structure in Bulgaria's water sector, subject to heightened stakeholder scrutiny
- Codes of conduct, internal controls and risk management aligned with Veolia Group's governance framework

### Concession governance

- 2018 renegotiation guaranteed municipal ownership of all digital solutions and databases generated by the concession, alongside refinancing and full repayment of company debt
- Performance and investment commitments independently assessed by Bulgarian state institutions

### Regulatory relationship

- Ongoing engagement with the Sofia Municipality (as concession grantor) and Bulgaria's Energy and Water Regulatory Commission (KEVR), which sets tariffs and service standards

## Ratings and evidence

Sofiyska Voda does not publish a standalone ESG rating at the local company level; as with Sofia Water's Bulgarian peer utilities, its ESG performance is best benchmarked through the Veolia Group, whose ratings provide a reference point for the standards its Bulgarian operations are expected to meet.

- MSCI ESG Rating: A ("Average")
- Sustainalytics ESG Risk Score: ~21.5 (medium risk category)
- LSEG/Refinitiv ESG Score: 75/100
- Sofiyska Voda has ranked as the second-largest investor in the Bulgarian water sector after the state since 2017 — a proxy indicator of sustained capital commitment to infrastructure performance

## Regulatory compliance

Sofiyska Voda operates under overlapping EU, national and municipal regulatory frameworks covering water quality, environmental protection, tariff-setting and concession governance.

- Bulgarian Water Act: governs integrated water resource management, water quality, and pollution control obligations
- EU Water Framework Directive and Bathing Water Directive: shape environmental quality standards and monitoring obligations
- Energy and Water Regulatory Commission (KEVR): sets tariffs and service-quality standards; compliance is a condition of the concession
- Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD): as part of the Veolia Group, Sofiyska Voda's operational data feeds into Group-level disclosure required of large EU public-interest entities
  Continuous monitoring, infrastructure investment tracking, and periodic independent assessment by state institutions support the company's ability to demonstrate compliance and "value for money" under the concession.

## Recommendations

Localised, standalone ESG reporting: While Group-level Veolia ratings offer a useful proxy, Sofiyska Voda should publish a standalone annual sustainability report specific to its Sofia operations — covering water-loss trends, emissions, customer outcomes and investment delivery — to give local stakeholders (municipality, regulator, citizens) reporting that is not diluted by unrelated Group-wide activities.

Science-based, group-aligned climate targets: Building on the Kubratovo plant's Gold Standard certification, Sofiyska Voda should adopt explicit interim emissions-reduction milestones aligned with Veolia Group's broader climate commitments, with a clear roadmap for scaling energy recovery and renewable integration across other treatment facilities.

Formalised supplier and contractor ESG requirements: As with the procurement standards seen in more mature ESG programmes, Sofiyska Voda should formalise ESG criteria and periodic audits for its contractor base, particularly around network construction and rehabilitation works, to extend accountability across its supply chain.

Expanded circular economy and stakeholder engagement: The company should build on its existing sludge-to-biogas practice by exploring further reuse of treatment by-products, and should formalise its ad hoc participation in public forums (e.g., on flood risk) into a standing stakeholder engagement mechanism with the municipality, regulator, and civil society.

### Learning value

This case illustrates how ESG integration functions within a regulated, public-private utility concession — a governance form with limited precedent in Bulgaria. It shows learners how infrastructure investment, environmental performance, and governance transparency are shaped as much by concession-contract design and multi-stakeholder oversight (municipality, national regulator, and a multinational parent company) as by voluntary corporate ESG strategy, offering a useful contrast to purely corporate-driven ESG cases such as listed banks or industrial firms.
