## Company overview

**Name:** SONNENTOR Kräuterhandelsgesellschaft mbH

**Sector:** Organic food production (herbs, teas, spices and related organic products)

**Location:** Sprögnitz, Waldviertel, Lower Austria

**Size:** Family-run SME and organic pioneer; exports to more than 40 countries; sister companies in the Czech Republic and Romania

**Reporting framework:** Gemeinwohl-Ökonomie (Economy for the Common Good – GWÖ / Common Good Balance Sheet)

**Reporting Period:** 2023–2025 (GWÖ cycle)

SONNENTOR is an Austrian organic food producer that exports teas, spices and related organic products to more than 40 countries. Since the late 1980s the company has built its brand around organic agriculture rooted in the Waldviertel, a rural region in northern Lower Austria. Its long-term growth is closely tied to a model of responsible business conduct, with an emphasis on socially and environmentally responsible behaviour and on building the business on fair partnerships with employees, farmers, suppliers and regional stakeholders. Rather than treating sustainability as a separate CSR add-on, SONNENTOR positions it as a core part of the business model, publishing detailed, evidence-rich reporting through the Economy for the Common Good (GWÖ) framework.

## ESG integration

The integration approach translates values – common good, cooperation and fairness – into operational decisions that affect the value chain, investments and reporting. The case demonstrates how a values-based framework (GWÖ) becomes a working management tool of targets, measures, audits and reporting cycles, and how it generates multiple “data entry points” (KPIs, audits, certificates and calculations) that can be turned into a structured data-collection plan.

## Environmental

### Decarbonisation roadmap (“Mission Null-Emission”)

- Short-term anchor target: fossil-emission-free operations at the Herbquarter in Sprögnitz from 1 April 2028, with later rollout to other sites.
- Implementation logic links the target to monitoring routines, recurring annual greenhouse-gas (GHG) balancing, and prioritised measures, especially in energy and mobility.

### Value-chain (Scope 3) data improvement

- SONNENTOR explicitly recognises that a significant share of emissions lies upstream and downstream in the value chain and is working to improve the data basis in order to set realistic targets and measures.
- This reflects a common real-world challenge: Scope 3 is material but data is incomplete, and method choices, supplier data, transport assumptions and product-use/end-of-life modelling all affect results.

### Resource and product measures

- Packaging optimisation: an ongoing shift to more sustainable packaging materials since 2008, with a headline impact claim of approximately 400 tonnes of plastic saved through the transition.
- Palm-oil avoidance: the company states that it avoids palm oil in its products due to environmental concerns.
- Broader ecological aims pursued through energy reduction, environmentally friendly product choices, and reducing chemicals and impacts in packaging.

## Social

### Common-good and community orientation

- Sustainability is framed not only as ecological performance but as a contribution to biological farming, regional employment and wider societal benefit.
- Profits are reinvested into employment creation and long-term sustainable growth in the operating regions.
- Automation is deliberately limited in parts of production to keep value creation and meaningful work embedded in local communities, with employees and farmers treated as core stakeholders.

### Fair value chain and partnerships

- Strengthening fair working conditions across the supply chain, using social enterprises as suppliers, and supporting NGOs via donations.
- This positioning can be operationalised into stakeholder-oriented indicators such as the share of procurement from defined regional/organic suppliers, fairness criteria in supplier relationships, regional employment impact, cooperation with social enterprises, and evidence of stakeholder engagement.

## Governance

### Externally audited “Gemeinwohl” (GWÖ) reporting

- A structured, audited reporting approach under the Economy for the Common Good model, with a matrix-based evaluation logic and independent auditors/external experts verifying disclosures.
- A recurring data-update rhythm (a two-year cycle), with the GWÖ balance created through cross-department collaboration over months and reviewed in a multi-day audit.
- These governance arrangements enable data quality, traceability and credibility – linking environmental and social responsibility directly to the organisation’s governance structure and culture.

## Ratings and evidence

SONNENTOR’s GWÖ reporting works as a transparent scorecard: it provides a headline score together with a structured breakdown behind it.

- Overall score of 755 out of 1,000 for the reporting period 2023 to 2025 (headline performance score).
- A positive trend over time (for example, from 630 in 2011 to 755 in the current period).
- A percentage breakdown across the GWÖ matrix by stakeholder groups and themes (e.g. suppliers, employees, customers/partners and the societal environment), which can be presented as a transparent scorecard view.
- Particularly strong categories, with “Ecological impact of product” and “Meaning and social impact of products” reported at 100%.
- Validity signal: the audit testat is valid until 30 November 2027, strengthening trust in the current scoring information.
- Audited expansion across sister companies in the Czech Republic and Romania, which strengthens the cross-site governance story.

## Regulatory compliance

SONNENTOR combines values-based GWÖ reporting with formal certificates that act as compliance and assurance signals, reducing greenwashing risk because they show independent checks and management-system maturity.

- Organic certificate (BIO Zertifikat): verifying compliance with EU organic-farming rules across the product range.
- HACCP certificate: food-safety management (hazard analysis and critical control points).
- ISO 14001 certificate: a certified environmental-management system.
  These certificates form an “evidence layer” that can be linked to specific claims and KPIs (environmental management processes, traceability of organic compliance, structured risk controls). At the same time, because GWÖ is a values-driven framework, EU stakeholders such as CSRD-reporting buyers, banks and partners increasingly expect ESRS-style datapoints or at least an ESRS-like structure alongside it.

## Recommendations

### 1) Add an EU-framework “mapping layer”.

GWÖ reporting is strong for values-driven transparency, but a mapping layer would translate GWÖ categories into a small set of comparable ESG datapoints, clarify which datapoints align with EU expectations (ESRS/CSRD), and reduce friction when stakeholders request standardised figures.

### 2) Formalise the value-chain (Scope 3) data plan.

Since SONNENTOR openly states that Scope 3 is material and data quality needs improvement, the company should define data owners, establish quality tiers (estimated → supplier-reported → verified), set an improvement timeline, and document assumptions and boundaries consistently.

### 3) Build evidence packs for headline claims (anti-greenwashing).

For highly visible claims – plastic savings, palm-oil avoidance and emission targets – SONNENTOR should create simple evidence packs containing calculation methods and definitions, system boundaries and time periods, source documents and responsible data owners, verification steps, and a lightweight audit trail that can be reused for stakeholder requests.
