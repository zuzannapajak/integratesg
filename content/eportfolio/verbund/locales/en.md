## Company overview

**Name:** VERBUND AG

**Sector:** Utilities – electricity generation, transmission, trading and sales

**Location:** Vienna, Austria (hydropower plants across Austria and Bavaria, Germany)

**Size:** Austria’s largest electricity company; 2024 revenue of approximately €8.2 billion with around 4,400 employees; listed on the Vienna Stock Exchange (WBAG: VER) and a constituent of the leading ATX index; the Republic of Austria holds a statutory majority stake of 51%

**Reporting Year:** 2024 (Integrated Annual Report, prepared under CSRD and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards)

Founded in 1947, VERBUND is Austria’s leading energy company and one of the largest producers of electricity from hydropower in Europe. It covers around 40% of Austria’s electricity demand, and in 2024 roughly 96% of its generation came from renewable sources – predominantly hydropower, supported by wind and photovoltaics – with hydropower capacity of about 8.7 GW. The Group operates across the full energy value chain, from generation and transport through to international sales, and its high-voltage grid subsidiary Austrian Power Grid (APG) plays a central role in integrating renewables. Under CEO Michael Strugl, sustainability is embedded in the corporate strategy (“Mission V”) and reported through a fully integrated annual report combining financial and sustainability information.

## ESG integration

For VERBUND, ESG is not a separate initiative but the core of the business model: as a renewable-energy utility, its climate performance and its commercial performance are closely linked. The integration approach is systematic and embedded across strategy, generation, grid investment, risk management and reporting, and is externally verified through integrated CSRD/ESRS reporting.

## Environmental

### Renewable generation at the core

- Around 96% of 2024 electricity generation came from hydro, wind and solar power; the remaining share stems from thermal plants still required for grid stability and district heating.
- Early phase-out of fossil fuels: lignite ended in 2006, crude oil in 2015 and hard coal in 2020, making VERBUND an early mover among European utilities.
- EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) emissions were reduced by roughly 92% between 2005 and 2023.

### Net-zero pathway and climate governance

- Commitment to net-zero greenhouse-gas emissions by 2050 (at least a 90% reduction from a 2020 base year, with the elimination of residual emissions), and an operational Scope 1 and 2 target of a 90% reduction by 2040.
- Science-based emission-reduction targets covering operational and value-chain emissions, and a Climate Transition Plan integrated into the annual report.
- Transparent climate disclosure to CDP (formerly the Carbon Disclosure Project) every year since 2007.

### Capacity expansion, grid and green finance

- Continued build-out of renewable capacity (hydro, onshore wind and photovoltaics) and a hydrogen strategy to decarbonise hard-to-abate uses.
- A major grid-modernisation programme through APG (around €9 billion of investment over the ten years to 2034) to bring hydropower from the west of Austria to demand centres in the east.
- Green bonds (including a €500 million placement) financing renewable-energy and grid projects, governed by a Green Bond Framework with external review and annual impact reporting.

## Social

### Enabling customers’ decarbonisation

- Supply of TÜV-certified green electricity, documented through guarantees of origin, allowing industrial and commercial customers to reduce their Scope 2 emissions and substantiate renewable-energy claims under EU accounting rules.

### People and stakeholders

- Around 4,400 employees, with attention to occupational health and safety, skills development and responsible workforce management.
- Structured stakeholder engagement through the ESRS double-materiality assessment, involving both internal and external stakeholders in defining material topics.
- Supply-chain responsibility and human-rights due diligence embedded in procurement, in line with emerging EU requirements.

## Governance

### Integrated, audited sustainability governance

- Sustainability is reported within an integrated annual report under CSRD and the ESRS, based on a double-materiality assessment, with Supervisory Board and Audit Committee oversight of ESG reporting, the materiality assessment and the Climate Transition Plan.
- ESG is anchored in the corporate strategy “Mission V”, linking environmental and social responsibility directly to the governance structure and capital-allocation decisions.

### Ownership and market discipline

- A statutory majority public shareholding (Republic of Austria, 51%) combined with a listing on the Vienna Stock Exchange (ATX), subjecting VERBUND to both public-interest obligations and capital-market governance and disclosure standards.
- A Green Bond Framework with external second-party opinion and sustainability performance targets benchmarked against utility peers (e.g. via ISS ESG).

## Ratings and evidence

VERBUND is widely recognised by external ESG rating providers and is positioned among the stronger performers in the European utilities sector.

- MSCI ESG Rating: AAA, the top grade on MSCI’s scale.
- Sustainalytics ESG Risk Rating: approximately 18.7, placing VERBUND in the “Low Risk” category.
- EcoVadis: a Gold medal with a score of 75 out of 100, placing the Group in the top 5% of the more than 100,000 companies assessed worldwide.
- CDP: continuous climate disclosure since 2007.
- Index membership and peer benchmarking: constituent of the ATX (Vienna Stock Exchange); benchmarked in the top tier of European electric-utility peers on renewable-energy integration in ISS ESG assessments used for its green-bond targets.

## Regulatory compliance

VERBUND demonstrates comprehensive compliance across EU and national frameworks, aligning corporate strategy with the European Green Deal while meeting sector-specific regulation.

### EU regulatory framework

- CSRD and ESRS: sustainability information integrated into the 2024 Integrated Annual Report on the basis of a double-materiality assessment.
- EU Taxonomy: reporting of taxonomy-eligible and taxonomy-aligned revenue, capex and opex, with renewable generation as a core aligned activity.
- Alignment with the Paris Agreement, the European Green Deal and the “Fit for 55” package; sustainable-finance disclosures supporting its green-bond programme.

### National and sector regulation

- Electricity-market and grid regulation under the Austrian regulator E-Control, including unbundling requirements met through the grid subsidiary APG.
- Compliance with the Austrian Electricity Energy Crisis Contribution (EKB-S) windfall levy, which caps eligible electricity revenue and has been extended for collection periods running to 2030.
- Participation in the EU ETS and provision of guarantees of origin for certified green electricity.

## Recommendations

### 1) Strengthen and disclose value-chain (Scope 3) targets.

While operational (Scope 1 and 2) targets are ambitious and validated, VERBUND should continue to expand the coverage, granularity and disclosure of Scope 3 and financed/enabling emissions, with clear interim milestones so that stakeholders can track value-chain progress as rigorously as operational progress.

### 2) Set a credible pathway for residual thermal assets and hydrogen.

The remaining thermal generation (used for grid stability and district heating) and the planned transition of gas capacity to hydrogen should be underpinned by transparent timelines, cost assumptions and contingency plans, so that the net-zero commitment does not depend on technologies that are not yet fully mature.

### 3) Deepen nature- and water-related disclosure.

Given the central role of hydropower, VERBUND should expand quantified biodiversity, river-continuity and water metrics and move towards nature-related disclosure (e.g. TNFD-aligned), demonstrating how renewable expansion is reconciled with ecological protection.

### 4) Advance supply-chain ESG due diligence.

As EU supply-chain due-diligence requirements take effect, VERBUND should formalise systematic ESG audits and improvement programmes across its key suppliers, with transparent scorecards, to ensure the value chain matches the Group’s own sustainability standards.
